Progress timeline

A reusable step timeline that sizes itself to its parent and keeps the full flow at every width. The controls below are demo-only: they set the parent's width and the props the host passes in. Drag the stage's bottom-right corner, swipe or drag the track, click the arrows, or use the arrow keys on it.

    How it decides

    Sizing

    Width is measured from the parent with a ResizeObserver. 128px stays reserved at each edge; the space between is where dots rest.

    All steps fit at the minimum segment? They spread evenly, first dot on the left safe line, last on the right. Otherwise the segment is exactly the minimum and the track scrolls.

    Positioning

    First step: its dot 128px from the left. Final step: 128px from the right. In between: the current dot at 40% of the width, slid on if needed so the next step shows whole, never so far that the current label goes under a fade. Always clamped to the track's limits.

    Small parents

    Under 600px the minimum segment is its own setting (64px here, 128px from 600px up), so a small card can keep more steps in view.

    Narrow (the usable width, width − 2 × 128, holds fewer than two minimum segments): the edges become 64px safe areas and the current step centres, previous and next either side with their labels, while three minimum segments fit across the safe areas (five 64s in 320px).

    Compact

    When fewer than three labelled steps fit across the safe areas (under 512px at a 128px minimum, under 320px at 64px), the labels go entirely. The dots and the line sit inside a 64px safe area each side, three across it (previous, current, next) as far as the minimum segment allows, and one line under them names the next step: “Up next – …”, with an optional info button. The current step centres as far as the track allows, with no blank space past the ends. The full timeline stays, scrollable.

    Overflow

    A fade (128px, 64px when compact) appears only on a side the track runs past. A 28px arrow sits centred in it, shown only when the timeline (its line, a dot or a label) is beneath its centre, never blank track; each click scrolls one segment.
